What Is a Break-Even Point?

The break-even point is the amount of sales a business needs to generate enough revenue to cover its costs. At break-even, the business has approximately zero profit before considering other factors that may not be included in the calculation.

How Is Break-Even Calculated?

Break-even sales can be estimated by dividing fixed costs by the contribution earned from each sale.

Contribution per sale is the selling price minus the variable cost associated with making or delivering that sale.

For example, if a product sells for $50 and costs $20 in variable expenses, the contribution is $30 per sale.

Why Is Break-Even Analysis Useful?

Break-even analysis can help business owners understand how many sales are needed to cover expenses. It can also be useful when evaluating pricing, costs, advertising plans, and revenue goals.

What Is Contribution Margin?

Contribution margin shows how much of each dollar of sales remains after variable costs. That remaining amount can then contribute toward fixed costs and profit.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does break-even mean?

Break-even means your revenue is approximately equal to the costs included in the calculation, resulting in approximately zero profit.

What is a variable cost?

A variable cost generally changes as the number of sales changes. Examples can include product costs, packaging, transaction fees, and other expenses directly associated with each sale.

What is a fixed cost?

A fixed cost generally does not change directly with each sale. Examples can include certain subscriptions, rent, software, insurance, or other recurring business expenses.

Can I use this calculator for an online business?

Yes. You can use this calculator for many types of businesses, including ecommerce stores, digital products, services, subscriptions, and other businesses where costs and sales can be estimated.

What if my variable cost is higher than my selling price?

If your variable cost is equal to or higher than your selling price, each sale does not generate enough contribution to cover fixed costs. The calculator will identify this situation rather than displaying a misleading break-even number.
